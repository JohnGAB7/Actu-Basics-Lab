Statistics & probability · S1.01

How many people live in Paris? What counts as data?

2.1 million, 11 million or 13.3 million: which figure describes Paris? All three can be correct. It depends on the question and the definition.

S1.014–6 minScript approved by JG on 8 November 2026

Learning objectives

  • Identify the main concept and its definitions.
  • Interpret examples while keeping their assumptions in view.
  • Check your understanding with an exercise.

1. A statistical question

“How old is my neighbour?” has one answer. “How old are the people on my street?” produces answers that vary from person to person.

A statistical question concerns a set of observations whose values vary and whose variation we want to describe or understand.

2. Data are measurements

A data point answers a specific question, relies on a definition of what is being counted and comes from a measurement made by someone, at a given date, using a method.

Data = a recorded answer to a defined and measured question.

3. Three definitions of Paris

France's official statistics office, Insee, reports three figures for the 2023 census, each using a different geographic boundary:

BoundaryPopulation
Paris municipality2,103,778
Urban unit (continuous built-up area)11,013,774
Functional area (commuting zone)13,320,752

The functional area has about 6.3 times the population of the municipality. These figures do not contradict one another: they answer three different definitions.

Source: Insee, 2023 population census. Each figure is exact for the boundary stated.

4. The habit to keep

Before comparing two numbers, ask: which question? Which definition? Who measured it, and when?
