Un milliard de secondes, c'est combien ? Ordres de grandeur

A million seconds is about eleven and a half days. What about a billion seconds?

M0.02Source script approved by JG · 8 November 2026Lesson transcript and learning material

Lesson and narration

Each sequence shows the on-screen material and its corresponding narration. Exercises reveal the correction after you have had time to think.

Sequence 1 · Accroche
A million seconds is about 11.6 days
Read the narration

A million seconds is about eleven and a half days. What about a billion seconds?

Sequence 2 · Exercice
[CHALLENGE] A billion seconds: 12 days, 3 years or 32 years?

4-second pause
Read the narration

Pick one before we go on: twelve days, three years, or thirty-two years?

Sequence 3 · Chapitre
1. Counting in powers of 10
Read the narration

To answer, let's count the zeros.

Sequence 4 · Étapes
Each step multiplies by a thousand

  • A thousand = 1,000 = 10³

  • A million = 1,000,000 = 10⁶

  • A billion = 1,000,000,000 = 10⁹

Read the narration

A thousand is a one followed by three zeros: ten to the power three. A million, six zeros: ten to the six. A billion, nine zeros: ten to the nine. At each step, you multiply by a thousand.

Sequence 5 · Formule
A billion = a thousand millions

Formula: 10⁹=10³× 10⁶
Read the narration

So a billion is a thousand millions. If a million seconds is eleven and a half days, a billion is a thousand times longer.

Sequence 6 · Chiffre
31.7 years for a billion seconds

Note: 10⁹ s ÷ (365.25 × 86,400 s per year) ≈ 31.7 years.
Read the narration

The answer to the challenge: a billion seconds is almost thirty-two years. The words differ by one letter, but the gap is enormous.

Sequence 7 · Chapitre
2. Scientific notation
Read the narration

To write very large and very small numbers without counting zeros, we use scientific notation.

Sequence 8 · Formule
A number from 1 to under 10, times a power of 10

Formula: 45,000=4.5× 10⁴

0.003=3× 10⁻³
Read the narration

You write a number that is at least one and less than ten, multiplied by a power of ten. Forty-five thousand is four point five times ten to the four: the decimal point moved four places. Zero point zero zero three is three times ten to the minus three: a negative power for numbers smaller than one.

Sequence 9 · Chapitre
3. Comparing by order of magnitude
Read the narration

The main benefit: comparing two quantities at a glance.

Sequence 10 · Étapes
Two fictional budgets

  • Project A: 2 million euros, or 2 × 10⁶

  • Project B: 3 billion euros, or 3 × 10⁹

  • 9 − 6 = 3 orders of magnitude, a factor of about 1,000

Read the narration

Take two fictional budgets. Project A costs two million euros, two times ten to the six. Project B costs three billion, three times ten to the nine. Between the powers there are three steps: three orders of magnitude, meaning a factor of around a thousand.

Sequence 11 · Chiffre
×1500 between the two budgets

Note: 3 × 10⁹ ÷ (2 × 10⁶) = 1,500.
Read the narration

The exact ratio is fifteen hundred. The order of magnitude already pointed to a gap of about a thousand: these two projects are not in the same league.

Sequence 12 · Étapes
The coefficient or the power?

  • 2 million = 2 × 10⁶

  • 3 million = 3 × 10⁶: same order of magnitude

  • 3 billion = 3 × 10⁹: three orders of magnitude higher

Read the narration

Compare. Two million and three million have the same power, ten to the six: they're the same order of magnitude, only the coefficient changes. Three billion is ten to the nine: three steps higher. The coefficient fine-tunes, the power makes the real jump.

Sequence 13 · Message
False friend: in French, billion = 10¹²

Note: In English, a billion is 10⁹; in French, a « billion » is a thousand times more.
Read the narration

A classic trap when reading the foreign press: in French, the word billion means a million million, ten to the twelve. The English billion is called a milliard. One translation slip, and the figure is off by a factor of a thousand.

Exercise · 14
Write 52 million in scientific notation

6-second pause
Read the narration

Your turn. How do you write fifty-two million in scientific notation?


  • 52,000,000: put the decimal point after the first digit

  • 5.2: the point moved back 7 places




Answer: 5.2 × 10⁷
Read the narration

Fifty-two million is fifty-two followed by six zeros. Put the decimal point after the first digit: five point two. It moved back seven places. The answer: five point two times ten to the seven.

Sequence 16 · Étapes
Key takeaways

  • Thousand, million, billion: ×1,000 at each step

  • Scientific notation: a number from 1 to under 10 × 10ⁿ

  • To compare: look at the powers first

Read the narration

Key takeaways. Thousand, million, billion: multiply by a thousand at each step. In scientific notation, a number of at least one and less than ten, times a power of ten. And to compare two quantities, look at the powers first.
