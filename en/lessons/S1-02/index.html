Statistics & probability · S1.02

Who is this number about? Population, unit, variable

“People in France sleep 7 hours a night.” Who exactly are we talking about? And what was measured? To understand a statistic, identify three building blocks.

S1.025–7 minScript approved by JG on 8 November 2026

Learning objectives

  • Identify the main concept and its definitions.
  • Interpret examples while keeping their assumptions in view.
  • Check your understanding with an exercise.

1. The three building blocks

  • Population: the full set of elements being studied.
  • Unit: each element observed, such as a person, household or company.
  • Variable: what is measured on each unit.
Population → observed units → measured variable

2. Read beyond the headline

“People in France sleep 7 hours a night.” Does the population include everyone, adults only or children? The unit is probably one person. The variable is sleep duration, but was it self-reported or measured by a device?

A short headline can hide the population and measurement method. Without them, two figures may look comparable when they are not.

3. Changing the unit changes the result

Consider a fictional village with three households:

HouseholdPeopleMonthly income
A1€2,000
B4€4,000
C2€3,000

The village has 7 residents and total monthly income of €9,000.

Average per household = €9,000 ÷ 3 = €3,000
Average per resident = €9,000 ÷ 7 ≈ €1,286

The result changes with the unit: household or person. Comparing an average per household with an average per person would be meaningless.

4. Check the three building blocks

Exercise

“30% of middle-school pupils had a phone before age 11.” Which interpretation is correct?
