Statistics & probability · S1.04

How many unemployed? Where numbers come from

How many people are unemployed in France? The answer depends on who is counting, and how.

S1.04Source script approved by JG · 8 November 2026Lesson transcript and learning material

Lesson and narration

Each sequence shows the on-screen material and its corresponding narration. Exercises reveal the correction after you have had time to think.

Sequence 1 · Accroche
How many unemployed people in France? It depends who's counting.
Read the narration

How many people are unemployed in France? The answer depends on who is counting, and how.

Sequence 2 · Chapitre
1. Four ways to collect data
Read the narration

There are four main ways to produce data.

Sequence 3 · Étapes
The four main sources

  • Census: ask everyone

  • Survey: ask a sample

  • Administrative records: record to manage

  • Sensors and platforms: keep traces

Read the narration

A census asks the whole population. A sample survey asks only a sample, chosen to represent it. Administrative records register people in order to run a service: taxes, births and deaths, registrations. Finally, sensors and platforms keep traces: cars going by, purchases, log-ins.

Sequence 4 · Étapes
One strength, one limitation

  • Census: complete, but heavy and costly

  • Survey: fast, but a margin of error

  • Records: huge, but defined by administrative rules

  • Sensors: continuous, but only where installed

Read the narration

Each source has a strength and a limitation. A census is complete, but heavy and costly to run. A survey is fast, but it has a margin of error, and some people don't answer. Administrative records are huge and already available, but they only count what their rules ask them to count. Sensors measure continuously, but only where they're installed. Platforms, for their part, only see their own users.

Sequence 5 · Message
What isn't collected doesn't show up in the data
Read the narration

Remember the general idea: every way of collecting data leaves its mark on the number. What isn't collected simply doesn't show up in the data.

Sequence 6 · Chapitre
2. Unemployment: survey or register?
Read the narration

Back to unemployment, which is measured in two different ways.

Sequence 7 · Étapes
Two ways of counting

  • Insee: a survey, ILO definition

  • No job, available, actively looking

  • France Travail: a register of jobseekers

Read the narration

On one side, Insee, the French statistics office, surveys a sample of households and applies the definition of the International Labour Organization. Under that definition, a person is unemployed if they have no job, are available to work, and are actively looking for one. On the other side, France Travail, the public employment service, keeps a register: it counts the people who have signed up, under its own rules.

Sequence 8 · Chiffre
2.7 million unemployed people under the ILO definition, Q2 2026

Note: Insee, Labour Force Survey: 2,677,000 people, seasonally adjusted.
Read the narration

According to Insee's survey, France had two point seven million unemployed people under the ILO definition in the second quarter of twenty twenty-six.

Sequence 9 · Message
A registered jobseeker isn't always ILO-unemployed, and vice versa

Note: Insee: 3.3 million people registered with France Travail in category A, Q2 2026 (France excluding Mayotte).
Read the narration

The number of people registered with France Travail is different: three point three million in category A, in the same quarter. That's not a mistake: the two figures don't measure the same thing. Someone who worked even one hour in the reference week isn't unemployed under the ILO definition, even if they stay registered. And someone actively looking for work without registering can be counted by the survey, but not by the register.

Exercise · 10
Where do these numbers come from? Births this year. Trust in scientists. Traffic on the ring road at 8 a.m.

6-second pause
Read the narration

Your turn. Where do these three numbers come from? The number of births this year. The share of people who trust scientists. The number of cars on the ring road at eight in the morning.


  • Births: administrative records, the civil register

  • Trust: a survey, with a margin of error

  • Traffic: sensors, only where there are some




Answer: A register, a survey, sensors
Read the narration

Births come from administrative records, the civil register: every birth is declared at the town hall. Trust in scientists comes from a survey: you can't ask everyone, hence a margin of error. Traffic comes from sensors on the road: it only counts the places that are equipped.

Sequence 12 · Étapes
What to remember

  • Ask: who collected it, and how?

  • Each source has a strength and a limitation

  • Two sources, two definitions: two numbers

Read the narration

Let's recap. When you see a number, ask who collected it, and how. Each source has a strength and a limitation. And two sources that count with two different definitions give two different numbers, without either of them being wrong.
