Statistics pathway · Level 1

Learn to read, describe and question data.

The first level builds the foundations of statistical reasoning: define what is being studied, assess how data were produced, describe distributions and explore relationships without jumping to causal conclusions.

What you will be able to do

From a number to a defensible statistical statement.

At the end of Level 1, you should be able to explain where a number comes from, what it measures, whom it describes, how reliable it is, and what can—and cannot—be concluded from it.

01 · Define the study

Distinguish statistical questions, populations, observational units, variables and measurement scales.

02 · Assess data quality

Identify sampling, nonresponse, measurement, recording and comparability problems.

03 · Describe distributions

Summarise a variable with tables, graphs, centre, spread, quantiles and robust summaries.

04 · Explore relationships

Compare groups, use cross-tabulations and scatter plots, interpret association and recognise confounding.

Mathematical level : Basic arithmetic, fractions, percentages and reading simple graphs. Algebra and probability will be introduced when needed; formal inference belongs to later levels.

Data literacy

Before calculating, understand what the data represent and how they were produced.

  • Statistical questions and data
  • Population, unit and variable
  • Variable types and scales
  • Data sources and collection
  • Sampling and selection bias
  • Observational studies and experiments
  • Missing data, errors and changing definitions

Describing one variable

Turn raw observations into a faithful description of a distribution.

  • Frequency tables
  • Bar charts and histograms
  • Distribution shape
  • Mean and median
  • Quantiles and box plots
  • Range and spread
  • Variance and standard deviation
  • Outliers and z-scores

  • Correlation

  • Changes over time

How to study this level

Understand

Start from the question and the data context before using a formula.

Formalise

Write definitions, units, assumptions and the quantity being described.

Practise and critique

Calculate, visualise, interpret, check limitations and explain the result in plain language.
